abstract: We present initial results from extremely well-resolved 3D magnetohydrodynamical simulations of idealized galaxy clusters, conducted using the AthenaPK code on the Frontier exascale supercomputer. These simulations explore the self-regulation of galaxy groups and cool-core clusters by cold gas-triggered active galactic nucleus (AGN) feedback incorporating magnetized kinetic jets. We find that the simulated clusters are self-regulating, with cluster cores staying at a roughly constant thermodynamic state for billions of years. Strong magnetic fields generally promote cold gas formation and allow cold gas out to much larger clustercentric radii than simulations with weak or no fields, and AGN feedback significantly increases the strength of magnetic fields at the cluster center.

summary: We present the XMAGNET suite of exascale MHD simulations of galaxy clusters run on Frontier, showing that magnetized, cold-gas-triggered AGN feedback self-regulates cluster cores while strongly shaping the extent and morphology of the cold gas.
