JWST at a glance

The James Webb Space Telescope was launched on 25 December 2021 and observes the sky in the infrared, from 0.6 to 28 µm. Its 6.5 m primary mirror is made of 18 gold-coated beryllium hexagons and collects about six times as much light as Hubble's. It orbits the Sun at the Sun–Earth L2 point, about 1.5 million km from Earth, behind a five-layer sunshield that keeps the telescope below about 50 K so that its own heat does not drown out the faint infrared signal. Infrared light is what lets it see through dust, and see the light of the earliest galaxies, which the expansion of the universe has stretched to these wavelengths.
