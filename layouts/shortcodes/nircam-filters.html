The filter bandpasses

A filter is a band centered on a wavelength λ with width Δλ, so its spectral resolution is R = λ/Δλ: about 4 for the wide filters, 10 for the medium ones and 100 for the narrow ones. Wide filters collect the most light; narrow ones isolate a single emission line. Bands in blue belong to the short-wavelength channel and bands in orange to the long-wavelength channel, and every observation uses one filter from each. Move the slider to redshift some familiar lines, which land at λrest(1 + z), and see which filters catch them.

Reading a filter name
F filter number nominal wavelength in units of 0.01 µm (150 = 1.50 µm); very wide filters can sit well off it W wide (R ∼ 4)  ·  M medium (∼10)  ·  N narrow (∼100)  ·  W2 extra-wide, roughly twice a wide filter
